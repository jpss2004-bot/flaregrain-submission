Finding 1 · BASS-II B1 and Saffire I-1

Comparing two airflow shutoff observations

Start with a question: How long did the flame take to go out after airflow stopped? Both observations describe prompt extinction in their respective tests. The cited passages give no elapsed time from shutoff to extinction in seconds.

  • BASS-II B1, sample 147. The flame went out quickly after airflow was stopped. The table gives a qualitative observation. NASA BASS-II summary, Table A.1, PDF p. 111 ↗
  • Saffire I-1, wide SIBAL fabric in concurrent flow. The investigators describe the flame going out immediately when airflow stopped at the end of the run. The 420 seconds is the test duration up to shutoff. NASA Saffire results, PDF pp. 14–16 ↗

LimitsBASS-II B1 used PMMA film with opposed flow. Saffire I-1 used cotton–fiberglass fabric with concurrent flow. Keep the conditions beside each observation when comparing them.

Finding 2 · BASS-II · NASA source checked

BASS-II: an acrylic rod kept burning at lower oxygen in microgravity

What NASA reports In the Burning and Suppression of Solids – II (BASS-II) experiment on the International Space Station, NASA researchers burned 24 PMMA (acrylic) rods in concurrent, stagnation-point flow and measured the conditions at which the flames blew off or quenched. For a ¼-inch PMMA rod, NASA reports:

BASS-II ¼-inch PMMA rod: oxygen values NASA reports
QuantityValueCondition
Steady flame spreading observed in microgravity (BASS-II)13.6% oxygen¼-inch PMMA rod, concurrent (stagnation-point) flow
Blowoff limit in normal gravity (1 g)16% oxygenthe same ¼-inch rod
Oxygen margin of safety−2.4 percentage points of oxygenthe difference between the two values above

NASA’s conclusion on the same slide is that normal-gravity flammability limits for this material should be “de-rated by at least that much”. The slide also notes that the minimum of the flammability boundary lies at low flow speeds, on the order of diffusive flows.

Limits
  • One material, one geometry and one flow: a ¼-inch PMMA rod in concurrent, stagnation-point flow. It is not a general rule for other materials, thicknesses or opposed flow.
  • The slide reports steady spreading at 13.6% oxygen. It does not say that 13.6% is the lowest oxygen level at which the rod can burn in microgravity.
  • The slide does not state the ambient pressure or describe how the 1 g blowoff limit was measured.
  • Later BASS-II publications were not checked against these figures.

Finding 3 · FLEX test 114

Reading an unverified pressure in FLEX

What NASA reports NASA’s Flame Extinguishment Experiment lists 0 mmHg for ambient pressure in test 114, run C11M201. The PSI-69 experimental table and NASA’s technical report both print zero. Those entries establish what was reported. The actual chamber pressure and the reason for the zero remain unverified.

Flaregrain reading Keep the printed 0 mmHg visible as reported, unverified. Leave the numeric pressure unset and omit this run from pressure charts and threshold filters. For example, a search for runs below 8 psi would otherwise treat this entry as a confirmed low-pressure measurement. The measurements from nearby runs apply to those runs only.

Finding 4 · Saffire I-1 and BASS-II B1

Reading a disputed measurement in Saffire I-1

Flaregrain marks a field as disputed when two sources report values that cannot be reconciled from the evidence available. For the Saffire I-1 sample thickness, start with the number and the unit in each original source.

  • Run 1-1 lists the cotton–fiberglass sample thickness as 0.37 mm. NASA investigator presentation, Table I, PDF page 29 ↗
  • Row S1 lists 0.37 under the heading Sample Thickness (cm). NASA PSI-98, Saffire-I experimental table, row S1 ↗

Flaregrain reading Check the unit even when the printed numbers match. A literal reading of the CSV header would make that entry 3.7 mm. That calculation illustrates the discrepancy. The available records do not resolve which thickness to use, so keep the two entries visible and leave the comparison’s preferred thickness unset.

MissingA missing value is a separate case. In the BASS-II summary, Table A.1, PDF page 111 ↗, the B1 row describes rapid extinction after airflow stopped and gives no elapsed time for that interval. Show the observed outcome and leave the numeric delay unavailable.
